# Messaging canon

How Lupine Science describes itself and its results. Every public surface in
this repository (homepage, `/results/`, `/about/`, `/corrections/`,
`/llms.txt`, the feed, articles, films, booklets, the venture deck and the
READMEs) follows these rules. When a rule
here conflicts with an older style note, this file wins and the older note
should be rewritten.

`data/company.json` holds the canonical sentences below; surfaces generated by
`scripts/lib/discovery.mjs` read them from there.
`tests/discovery-copy.test.mjs` guards the generated front door, and
`tests/front-door-structure.test.mjs` pins its structure and word budgets
(§9).

## 1. Mission and positioning

- **Mission:** Make AI predictions of new materials checkable before a lab
  commits to them.
- **One-liner** (metadata descriptions, llms.txt summary, README first line):
  "Lupine Science measures where machine-learned models of atoms go wrong,
  against published references and pass/fail gates set in advance, and
  publishes every verdict, failures included."
- **Short description:** "Lupine Science is an early-stage, founder-led
  research company. We independently measure the errors of machine-learned
  interatomic potentials (MLIPs), test corrections against preregistered
  gates, record any amendment made after results are seen, and publish the
  verdict: pass, fail or abstain. Correction is a research question here, not
  a product: it has held only in narrow, stated cases. Nothing we have
  published is peer-reviewed yet."
- **Homepage headline:** "Before a lab spends a year on a material, know
  whether to trust the prediction."
- **Tagline:** "Evidence before claim."
- **Descriptor beside the name:** "measuring where AI models of materials go
  wrong".

## 2. Pillars

1. **Gates first, verdicts always.** Pass lines are written before a test
   runs, records are hash-locked, and the verdict is published whether it
   passes, fails or abstains. Amendments made after results are seen are
   labelled as such, and the original gate's verdict stays beside the amended
   one.
2. **Look inside the average.** A pooled MLIP error can hide where a model
   fails. Stratify (by functional, pair style, element) and pool with random
   effects before trusting a leaderboard number. Our own cautionary example: a
   pooled "1.8× gate efficacy" was withdrawn as a class-composition
   (Simpson's) artifact.
3. **Check it yourself.** Committed data, replication kits, and the LUPI
   browser viewer (a WebGPU-capable browser, no installation).
4. **Narrow results, scope attached.**

## 3. Audiences, in this order

1. MLIP builders and benchmark maintainers
2. Computational materials scientists and lab directors
3. HPC, security and legal reviewers
4. Press and analysts

Investors are reached through the contact line only, never as a lead
audience. The contact line, used only in contact blocks, is: "Research
partners, model builders, press and investors: alex@lupinesci.com." We do not
publish funding statements of any kind.

## 4. Voice

- **Scope first.** Say what was tested, on what, against which reference,
  before saying what it showed.
- **Failures beside passes.** A list of results that leaves out the failures
  is not a list of results.
- **Plain words.** Write for a careful reader outside the field. Define a term
  the first time it matters.
- **Results, not counters.** Counts of theorems, commits, articles or films
  are not evidence and never appear as headlines or stat rows.
- **We.** Write as "we" or "Lupine Science". Never "the team", agent persona
  names, or internal stage names.
- **AI assistance is disclosed; a human author is responsible** for every
  public summary.

Numbers rule: quote a figure only if it appears in a linked article or data
file, with its scope and qualifiers in the same sentence. Never strengthen a
claim when shortening it. If a qualifier is missing from the cited page, add
it there or drop the clause.

## 5. Claims we stand behind

Reuse these verbatim or shorten them; never strengthen them. Each carries its
qualifier wherever the number appears.

- **Round 4 (July 2026).** In four preregistered campaigns, no gate passed:
  three failed and one abstained. On a 30-path Li-ion migration-barrier
  panel, MACE-MP-0 small and medium, MACE-MPA-0 medium and CHGNet missed a
  40 meV gate (MAE 135.0–242.5 meV on completed paths). A lattice- and
  elastic-constant correction on rocksalt ionics and perovskites failed
  (ionics 0/4; perovskites 0/1 after a post-measurement amendment). Every
  selected Δ-correction made catalyst-adsorption holdout error worse. The
  magnetism campaign abstained: no available model had the spin physics it
  needed. Keep the rocksalt/perovskite result separate from the cubic-metal
  elastic result.
- **fcc error field.** For nine fcc metals, an error field fitted to three
  other observables predicted the never-fitted (110) surface-energy error of
  four foundation MLIPs at r = 0.906 across 36 model–material cells
  (material-clustered 95% CI 0.82–0.96; permutation null centred near 0.44;
  significant for three of the four models).
- **Energy-only correction.** Used as an energy-only correction beside CHGNet,
  the field cut blind (110) surface-energy error from 9.7% to 1.5% on nickel
  and from 28.0% to 13.7% on copper: one model, two metals, one facet.
- **Elastic ceiling.** On 16 elemental cubic metals with four MatPES MLIPs
  (128 cases; r2SCAN targets approximate), one bias direction per functional
  transfers out of sample. When the held-out reference sets how far to
  correct, MAE falls from 17.84 to 10.36 GPa: a ceiling, not a deployable
  correction. A correction that works without seeing the answer is still
  open, and derived moduli are uncertified.
- **Small cell.** On the same 16-metal panel, 1×1×1 TensorNet/PBE cells
  matched 3×3×3 cells for elastic-constant error (14.55 vs 14.61 GPa MAE),
  while a global correction made the result worse (63.40 GPa).
- **Rankings.** Across 21 materials and 228 published reference values, four
  foundation MLIPs ranked surface energies almost correctly (Spearman
  0.88–1.00) while missing magnitudes by tens of percent. Apparent
  cross-model alignment sat inside a coupling-aware null, so we do not count
  it as a result.
- **October 2026 explainer.** Lead with its own conclusion: a retrospective
  analysis of previously examined data, in which a learned shared direction
  added little or no advantage over a common-direction baseline. No new
  physical validation is claimed.
- **Shared DFT anchors** (only on the shared-dft-anchors page and the Savings
  Stack booklet): projected accounting, no new DFT: 154 instead of 558
  projected DFT evaluations on 29 analyzable paths (72.4% fewer). The baseline
  is four separate runs; paths have 5–7 images, so the union covers most
  images and deduplication alone caps the saving near 73%. This figure does
  not appear on the homepage, in stat rows or in company facts.
- **Self-corrections.** The BCC/FCC "causal shield" was about 1.5% data
  contamination; a d-band signal was a sample-size artifact; four of seven
  registered Projection Law Round-2 predictions failed.
- **Lean.** "Lean 4 checks the arithmetic and stated inequalities behind
  published numbers; it does not check the physics, the reference data or the
  pipeline." Badge: "Lean-checked arithmetic". No bare theorem counts in
  heroes, stat rows, share text or summaries; a generated, dated count may
  appear once in a Lean section with that sentence attached. The 27/26
  example: "at the 10⁻⁴ precision used for checking, one cell's improvement
  rounds to zero, so we report 26 of 36 (27 of 36 at float64)."
- **TMS2027.** A poster abstract was accepted for TMS2027 (Orlando, 14–18
  March 2027). It is a conference presentation, not a paper. The accepted
  abstract described a universal error "hyper-ribbon"; our record no longer
  supports that, and the proceedings manuscript replaces it with narrower
  measurement claims.
- **Peer review.** "No Lupine paper has been peer-reviewed or accepted yet."
- **Tools.** atlas-distill implements standard fixed- and random-effects
  meta-analysis of correlation coefficients across independent groups (do not
  call it new). LUPI (lupi.live) opens structures and LAMMPS dump files in a
  WebGPU-capable browser with no installation. No atom-count or frame-rate
  claims.

### Retired

Do not use these anywhere, including as evidence in passing: "~70% of
dominant error directions are shared", "mean cos ≈ 0.69", "participation
ratio 1.1–2.0" as headline evidence, "559 potentials" as independent
potentials, theorem counts as headlines ("899 build-locked theorems, zero
sorry"), "> 0.8 cross-MLIP cosine", "one correction vector repairs all",
"universal barrier underprediction", "17.84 → 9.92 GPa", "44.4%", and a
class-aware correction with "zero no-harm violations" presented as usable.
Each retirement that was ever public is listed on `/corrections/`.

## 6. Replacement vocabulary

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "Verified AI predictions", "makes AI predictions trustworthy", "the trust layer" as a product | "independent, preregistered measurement of MLIP error" ("The Trust Layer" may remain as the title of the founding-thesis essay) |
| "corrects that error at runtime as a physical field beside the model" | "Correction is a research question here, not a product: it has held only in narrow, stated cases." |
| "seals its claims as machine-checked Lean 4 theorems" | the Lean sentence in section 5 |
| "proof pack" | "evidence record" |
| "certificate(s)" for physical properties | delete |
| "independently reviewed" with no named third party | "internally reviewed (AI-assisted)" or "checked by a separate exact-arithmetic implementation" |
| "measured" for the 72.4% figure | "projected" |
| "correction license" in prose | "correction clearance" (code identifiers may stay) |
| internal model-review stage names | "model review stage"; "the founder reviews every public summary" |
| "Accelerating materials discovery", "Unlocking the materials that build the future" | "measuring where AI models of materials go wrong"; "Evidence before claim." |

Partners: never describe a lab, company, agency or person as a partner,
pilot or collaborator unless they have agreed to it publicly. Citing a lab's
published work as a source is fine. An open invitation ("we want to work with
labs that…") is fine.

Climate is one lane among several (solid electrolytes and batteries,
catalysts, frameworks and MOFs, environmental and climate materials, alloys).
Literature estimates of climate impact appear only attributed to their source
in the body, never as a Lupine outcome or in a headline.

## 7. Status labels

**Document status** (one per page, plain label):

- **Published**: current statement on a Lupine site; not peer-reviewed;
  corrections dated in the body.
- **Working draft**: public for discussion, may change, not for citation.
- **Direction**: describes where the work is aimed; not a result.
- **Archived**: superseded; keeps its URL with a dated banner and noindex;
  removed from indexes, feed, sitemap, llms.txt and the homepage.

**Result status** (inline, in Evidence Status lines, tables and ledgers):
Supported · Open · Failed · Abstained · Refuted by us · Self-corrected ·
Withdrawn.

"Lean-checked arithmetic" is a badge, never a status. A Lean-checked
empirical claim is Open unless an empirical test supports it. A same-engine
check alone is Open, noted "same-engine only".

## 8. Corrections

When a public claim is retired, add it to `data/corrections.json` (rendered at
`/corrections/`) in the form "Was / Now / Why", one line each, with the date
retired. Keep it neutral, factual and short. Removing an overreach is fine;
removing a disclosed failure is not. Entries are added, never dropped.

## 9. One page, one question, and word budgets

A first-time visitor should understand Lupine in ten seconds and reach every
result, failure and correction in one click. Less text everywhere; nothing
hidden.

- **Homepage**: what is this, and is it real? The H1, the one-liner and the
  mission verbatim, two buttons (every result; LUPI), three scoped highlights,
  one row of Direction lanes, the next public appearance, the footer.
  At most 350 visible words, not counting the nav and footer. No jargon on
  the first screen.
- **`/results/`**: what held and what did not? Every tested row, failures and
  abstentions included, at most 45 words per row with its scope qualifier
  kept; what we cannot claim; the Lean scope sentence.
- **`/about/`**: who, how, contact. At most 450 visible words.
- **`/corrections/`**: what we retracted. At most 60 words per entry.
- **`/articles/`**: what can I read? At most five pieces under Start here,
  then one line per article (title, date, status; no summaries), Direction
  folded.
- **READMEs**: one screen, at most 45 lines, with a "More" list of links to
  `docs/` for anything moved.

Move detail between pages; never delete evidence. Every failure, abstention
and qualifier must stay on `/results/` or in its article.
