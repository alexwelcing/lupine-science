# Midwest 2076 — World Bible

Status: authored brand canon for speculative artwork; production planning only  
Edition: 1.0 · 2026-08-24

> Speculative worldbuilding artwork informed by materials culture. Not a forecast, engineering proposal, validated material claim, scientific reference, simulation result, or evidence of a real deployment.

## Core proposition

Midwest 2076 is a repair civilization.

The future is not announced by gadgets, skylines, or molecular decoration. It is inferred from the way ordinary institutions maintain unfamiliar material systems through humid heat, lake ice, drought, flood sediment, road salt, freight abrasion, and long public use.

The molecule is an invisible cause. The visible story is a changed wall, seam, spool, channel, threshold, workbench, public room, or regional exchange.

## Brand relationship

Midwest 2076 extends Lupine Science without pretending to document Lupine Science deployments.

- Lupine Science voice: evidence before claim; calm, precise, ambitious, trustworthy.
- Canon tagline remains: **Unlocking the materials that build the future.**
- Midwest 2076 is always labeled as speculative artwork or prospectus worldbuilding.
- A material premise may begin a story, but the image must show consequences rather than explain chemistry.
- No image is evidence for performance, feasibility, safety, adoption, or a real community.

## Canon laws

### 1. Maintenance is the hero

Every system has removable parts, service access, residue, storage for replacements, and an understandable failure state. Immaculate infrastructure is off-world; Midwest 2076 is touched, patched, salted, stained, folded, and reopened.

### 2. The future changes topology

A new material behavior must remove a present constraint and change the arrangement of space. New cladding on a familiar building is not enough.

Required chain:

1. material premise;
2. removed constraint;
3. topology change;
4. scale discontinuity;
5. operational consequence;
6. changed labor;
7. changed civic or domestic space;
8. regional consequence;
9. camera-visible proof.

### 3. Institutions outlast products

Libraries, county workshops, freight courts, water points, repair schools, clinics, winter rooms, foundries, cooperatives, and public archives carry the world. Consumer devices do not.

### 4. Weather is evidence, not spectacle

Weather appears as ice at a gasket, salt at a lower seam, grit in a clip, water marks on a wall, folded shade, condensation on an optical port, or clothing drying in a refuge room. Sky is subordinate and normally occupies no more than fifteen percent of an exterior frame.

### 5. Regional specificity is operational

The Midwest is freshwater, freight, flat wind, freeze-thaw, humid heat, black soil, glacial aggregate, industrial reuse, county-scale government, and cooperative maintenance. Barns, tractors, flags, silos, and picturesque fields are not sufficient regional evidence.

Kentucky is treated as an **Ohio Valley hinge/offshoot**, not silently relabeled as geographically Midwestern. Its karst, livestock, tobacco-barn fabric, and county water logistics connect it to the wider inland material economy.

### 6. Public use is ordinary

People may read, wait, repair, move freight, shelter, dry clothing, inspect seams, or share tools. They are never futuristic lifestyle models. Accessibility, waiting, storage, mess, and repeated use remain visible.

### 7. Authority must be earned in-frame

A mechanically coherent relationship is more important than atmosphere. If the system depends on a removable cartridge, open seam, matching replacement, separate clean and dirty paths, or continuous train-length skin, that relationship must be unmistakable without a caption.

## Visual grammar

### Palette

Use the Lupine brand palette for layouts and publication surfaces:

- paper `#faf9f6`
- ink `#16171d`
- ink-soft `#4c4e58`
- indigo `#3d4db3`
- ochre `#8a5e1f`

World imagery uses a related material palette rather than literal brand-color grading:

- bone ceramic and pale mineral foam;
- graphite composite and peat-black joints;
- oxidized copper-green fittings;
- weathered galvanized or cold silver metal;
- dark clay and wet mineral gray;
- one restrained vermilion, amber, sulfur-yellow, indigo, or red functional accent.

Avoid teal-orange cinema grading, cyan-on-black interfaces, purple-blue gradients, lush eco-utopian green, pure white futurism, and uniform beige desaturation.

### Materials

Canonical material families:

- porous mineral bodies that grade from structure to filtration;
- flexible structural skins with reversible load-spreading seams;
- ceramic combs, cartridges, exchange blocks, and gasketed guide segments;
- woven mineral fibers whose geometry changes passively;
- refractory shells repaired through folds, clips, patches, and tension nodes;
- copper-gray clean-side manifolds separated from dirty process paths;
- reclaimed black frames that persist while replaceable surfaces circulate.

The same joint grammar should recur across scales. A clip seen in a macro study should plausibly belong to the system shown in a landscape or civic interior.

### Image character

- Matter-of-fact large-format documentary photography.
- Believable gravity, enclosure, span, scale, joints, and wear.
- Neutral northern or overcast light; functional work lights only.
- Eye-level, transverse, oblique, or compressed telephoto views more often than heroic aerials.
- Infrastructure or occupied section fills at least two thirds of the frame.
- Two focal points: primary system plus a secondary proof such as a replacement cart, open slot, repair trace, or public threshold.
- Interiors include ceiling, floor, and at least two enclosing walls unless intentionally cropped as a material study.

### Recurring foreground evidence

Use at least two when relevant:

- matching removed and installed parts;
- reversible clips, pins, combs, clamps, gaskets, or course bands;
- carts, cradles, spools, wash racks, repair tables, or stored replacement bays;
- salt tide lines, soot, sediment, condensation, abrasion, patched strata, or hand-polished edges;
- separate public, clean-service, and dirty-service paths;
- ordinary belongings establishing use without portraiture.

## Institutional canon

### County material office

A small public institution that inventories reusable components, schedules shared repair equipment, maintains emergency stocks, and records material aging without screen-heavy command-center imagery.

### Repair school

Technical education occurs inside active infrastructure. Learners work on full-scale cartridges, skins, seams, acoustic cassettes, and water-control parts rather than clean demonstration models.

### Commons

A commons is an operating agreement made spatial: shared loading, storage, maintenance, refuge, testing, and access. It is never a landscaped plaza added beside machinery.

### Stewardship interval

Systems are designed around planned intervals when one bay, lane, cartridge, skin, or exchange block leaves service while neighbors remain active. These intervals are culturally ordinary and visually central.

### Material archive

An archive preserves failed parts, repair histories, samples, and local fabrication knowledge. It is workshop-adjacent and visibly handled, not a pristine specimen gallery.

## Social texture

The world should imply:

- municipal technicians, librarians, freight crews, farmers, clinic staff, fabricators, and residents sharing technical stewardship;
- repair knowledge as durable civic capacity;
- public rooms that serve daily life and seasonal refuge without becoming emergency-camp imagery;
- slow retreat, adaptation, and negotiated limits rather than effortless abundance;
- continuity with inherited buildings, roads, yards, and institutions;
- uneven conditions across the region without poverty spectacle or triumphalist rescue narratives.

Do not invent specific tribal, immigrant, labor, or community traditions without collaborators and source material from those communities. Generic visual markers are not a substitute for authorship.

## Time depth

Each scene belongs to one of four temporal modes:

1. **Ordinary operation** — the system is being used without ceremony.
2. **Stewardship interval** — a component is isolated, removed, washed, patched, or replaced.
3. **Seasonal conversion** — the system changes state for freeze, flood, drought, humid heat, or wind.
4. **After-use evidence** — wear, storage, residue, and occupancy reveal what happened without depicting the event.

A sequence should use at least three modes. Do not make every image a construction scene or every landscape a crisis.

## Geographic structure

Midwest 2076 is organized by connected operational territories rather than state stereotypes:

- Great Lakes shore: ice, corrosion, foundry heat, freshwater freight, temporary enclosures;
- prairie watershed: wind, soil moisture, snow distribution, shared field repair;
- inland river: sediment, navigation, flood passage, separation surfaces, public crossings;
- freight city: rail-barge transfer, acoustic edges, repair education, inhabited industrial boundaries;
- glacial quarry belt: graded mineral fabrication, flood components, local material loops;
- winter civic interior: thermal depth, daylight guidance, drying, refuge, public maintenance;
- Ohio Valley hinge: karst water, drought withdrawal, livestock logistics, county response, inherited rural fabric.

## Expansion doctrine

The next expansion is a 48-shot slate: eight connected territories with six evidence roles each. It deliberately fills current gaps in governance, changed labor, domestic life, maintenance failure, seasonal conversion, and cross-regional exchange.

The machine-readable production source is `expansion-slate.json`.

Required shot roles per territory:

1. `territory` — geography and inherited fabric;
2. `mechanism` — the minimum relationship that makes the material premise legible;
3. `ordinary-use` — the institution or household in daily operation;
4. `stewardship` — removal, cleaning, patching, storage, or replacement;
5. `regional-interface` — connection to freight, water, public service, or another territory;
6. `material-coda` — close evidence that repeats the system's joint and wear grammar.

## Sequence gate

A territory sequence passes only if an unlabeled viewer can infer:

- what present constraint has changed;
- which physical relationship enables the new topology;
- what people do differently;
- how the system fails and remains serviceable;
- why it belongs to this inland region;
- how the close material image belongs to the same system.

Shared atmosphere does not rescue a mechanically incoherent sequence.
