# 01 — Opposite streams

Classification: Speculative worldbuilding artwork informed by materials culture. Not a forecast, architectural proposal, engineering design, scientific reference, simulation result, or evidence of a real deployment.

Render a wide 16:9 cinematic public-works interior in a plausible near-future Midwestern municipal water hall. One mineral-rich brine line enters from the left through an open, serviceable pipe rack and removable cartridge bank; one PFAS-contaminated water line enters from the right through a physically separate sealed cartridge bank. The two liquid streams never mix. A shared central measurement spine crosses both lines without combining them. The mineral side terminates at a modest recovered-fraction vessel; the contaminant side terminates at a sealed transfer vessel leading to a contained pilot treatment cell. Make plumbing topology physically readable: visible elbows, isolation valves without text, removable cartridge housings, floor drains, access clearances, handrails, bolt patterns, maintenance carts, residue at joints, condensation, ordinary institutional wear. Human scale only through distant small workers or maintenance traces, never portraits. Materials: mineral-black structural steel, structural-cream ceramic housings, oxidized-copper transfer fittings, water-blue liquid glimpses, lichen-colored regenerated cartridge sleeves, muted evidence-red containment details. Overcast daylight through high clerestory windows, no dramatic sky, no glossy sci-fi, no neon, no molecule imagery, no generated text, labels, gauges, logos, diagrams, numbers, charts, control-screen gibberish, or implied commercial success. One dominant proof: opposite material streams sharing a test discipline while remaining separate.
