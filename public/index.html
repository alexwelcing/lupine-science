Evidence before claim

Before a lab spends a year on a material, know whether to trust the prediction.

Machine-learned potentials now screen batteries, catalysts and frameworks faster than any lab can test them, and a model can be confident and wrong. We measure where these models go wrong, against published references and pass/fail gates set in advance, and publish every verdict, failures included.

Our aim: make AI predictions of new materials checkable before a lab commits to them.

Where it has been tested

Results we have published, failures beside passes, each with the scope it holds in. They are not yet peer-reviewed. Other rounds, including Round 3, and our self-corrections are in the hypothesis ledger.

What we testedScope and numbersResult
Blind prediction of fcc (110) surface-energy error Nine fcc metals, four foundation MLIPs: an error field fitted to three other observables predicted the never-fitted (110) error at r = 0.906 across 36 model–material cells (material-clustered 95% CI 0.82–0.96; permutation null centred near 0.44; significant for three of the four models). Supported
Energy-only correction beside CHGNet Blind (110) surface-energy error fell from 9.7% to 1.5% on nickel and from 28.0% to 13.7% on copper: one model, two metals, one facet. Supported
Surface-energy rankings Across 21 materials and 228 published reference values, four foundation MLIPs ranked surface energies almost correctly (Spearman 0.88–1.00) while missing magnitudes by tens of percent. Supported
Shared error direction across models (21-material benchmark) Apparent cross-model alignment sat inside a coupling-aware null, so we do not count it as a result. Failed
Elastic-constant bias direction, 16 cubic metals Four MatPES MLIPs, 128 cases, r2SCAN targets approximate: one bias direction per functional transfers out of sample. When the held-out reference sets how far to correct, MAE falls from 17.84 to 10.36 GPa: a ceiling, not a deployable correction. A correction that works without seeing the answer is still open, and derived moduli are uncertified. Open
Small cells for elastic benchmarks On the same 16-metal panel, 1×1×1 TensorNet/PBE cells matched 3×3×3 cells for elastic-constant error (14.55 vs 14.61 GPa MAE). Supported
Global elastic correction on that panel A global correction made the result worse: 63.40 GPa MAE. Failed
Round 4: Li-ion migration barriers 30-path panel; MACE-MP-0 small and medium, MACE-MPA-0 medium and CHGNet missed a 40 meV gate (MAE 135.0–242.5 meV on completed paths). Failed
Round 4: lattice- and elastic-constant correction Rocksalt ionics and perovskites: ionics 0/4; perovskites 0/1 after a post-measurement amendment. Failed
Round 4: catalyst-adsorption Δ-correction Every selected Δ-correction made holdout error worse. Failed
Projection Law Round 2: registered predictions Four of seven registered predictions across two experiments failed and are reported as failures. Failed
Round 4: magnetism No available model had the spin physics the test needed. Abstained
Round 5: direction-gated barrier correction On the 30-path Li-ion panel, fitted on 12 disjoint training paths: all four models abstained at the direction gate, so no correction was applied. Abstained
Sparse-DFT anchor pilot (GPAW), 23 of 30 paths The preregistered 40 meV gate against published VASP barriers failed on path 7 (118.8 meV). An amendment written after that failure moved the gate to a same-engine check, which passed; against the published references the error was 693.0–705.7 meV. Same-engine only. Open

Where this is aimed

DirectionThese pieces describe where the work is aimed. They are not results. Each lane says what we have tested in it so far.

Solid electrolytes and batteries

Migration barriers decide how fast ions move through a solid electrolyte. Tested so far: on a 30-path Li-ion panel, none of the four foundation MLIPs we tested met our 40 meV gate (Round 4, Failed).

Catalysts

Adsorption energies decide which surfaces are worth making. Tested so far: every selected Δ-correction made catalyst-adsorption holdout error worse (Round 4, Failed).

Alloys

Tested so far: on fcc Cantor-subset alloys (CoCrNi, CoCrFeNi), our first correction made bulk-modulus error worse (median 9.1% → 16.9%; Failed). Our supported results are mostly on elemental metals; the 21-material ranking set includes two ordered intermetallics (B2-NiAl, L1₂-Ni₃Al).

Lean-checked arithmetic

Lean 4 checks the arithmetic and stated inequalities behind published numbers; it does not check the physics, the reference data or the pipeline.

One example, from the blind surface-energy test: at the 10⁻⁴ precision used for checking, one cell’s improvement rounds to zero, so we report 26 of 36 (27 of 36 at float64).

Recent notes

2026·10·21

Learning What an Error Pattern Can Tell Us

A retrospective analysis of previously examined data: errors stayed concentrated, but a learned shared direction added little or no advantage over a simple common-direction baseline. No new physical validation is claimed.

 2026·08·03

The Small Cell Held

On 16 cubic metals, 1×1×1 TensorNet/PBE cells matched 3×3×3 cells for elastic-constant error (14.55 vs 14.61 GPa MAE), while a global correction made the result worse (63.40 GPa).

 2026·07·19

Round 4: four gates, no pass

Four preregistered campaigns in July 2026: no gate passed. Three failed and one abstained; the records are public.
