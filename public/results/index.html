What held and what did not, each with its scope. Not peer-reviewed.

Every result, failures included

What we testedScope and numbersResult
Blind fcc (110) surface-energy error Nine fcc metals, four foundation MLIPs: a field fitted to three other observables predicted the never-fitted (110) error at r = 0.906 across 36 cells (material-clustered 95% CI 0.82–0.96; permutation null near 0.44; significant for three of the four models). Supported
Energy-only correction beside CHGNet Blind (110) surface-energy error fell from 9.7% to 1.5% on nickel and from 28.0% to 13.7% on copper: one model, two metals, one facet. Supported
Surface-energy rankings Across 21 materials and 228 published reference values, four foundation MLIPs ranked surface energies almost correctly (Spearman 0.88–1.00) while missing magnitudes by tens of percent. Supported
Shared error direction across models (21 materials) Apparent cross-model alignment sat inside a coupling-aware null, so we do not count it as a result. Failed
Elastic-constant bias, 16 cubic metals Four MatPES MLIPs, 128 cases, approximate r2SCAN targets: one bias direction per functional transfers out of sample. Sized by the held-out reference, MAE falls 17.84 → 10.36 GPa: a ceiling, not a deployable correction. Blind correction open; derived moduli uncertified. Open
Small cells for elastic benchmarks On the same 16-metal panel, 1×1×1 TensorNet/PBE cells matched 3×3×3 cells for elastic-constant error (14.55 vs 14.61 GPa MAE). Supported
Global elastic correction on that panel A global correction made the result worse: 63.40 GPa MAE. Failed
Cross-class correction on fcc alloys On CoCrNi and CoCrFeNi, a correction learned on elemental fcc metals made median bulk-modulus error worse: 9.1% to 16.9%. Failed
Round 4: Li-ion migration barriers 30-path panel; MACE-MP-0 small and medium, MACE-MPA-0 medium and CHGNet missed a 40 meV gate (MAE 135.0–242.5 meV on completed paths). Failed
Round 4: lattice- and elastic-constant correction Rocksalt ionics and perovskites: ionics 0/4; perovskites 0/1 after a post-measurement amendment. Failed
Round 4: catalyst-adsorption Δ-correction Every selected Δ-correction made holdout error worse. Failed
Round 4: magnetism No available model had the spin physics the test needed. Abstained
Projection Law Round 2: registered predictions Four of seven registered predictions across two experiments failed. The 90% conformal intervals reached 86.7% coverage, below the registered 90%. Failed
Round 5: direction-gated barrier correction On the 30-path Li-ion panel, fitted on 12 disjoint training paths: all four models abstained at the direction gate, so no correction was applied. Abstained
Sparse-DFT (GPAW) anchor pilot, 23 of 30 paths Preregistered 40 meV gate against published VASP barriers failed on path 7 (118.8 meV). A post-failure amendment moved the gate to a same-engine check, which passed; against published references the error was 693.0–705.7 meV. Same-engine only. Open

Not a gated test: in an October 2026 retrospective of previously examined data, errors stayed concentrated, but a learned shared direction added little or no advantage over a common-direction baseline. No new physical validation is claimed.

Other rounds, including Round 3, and our self-corrections are in the hypothesis ledger. Claims we have retired are in the corrections log.

What we cannot claim

  • That any correction we have tested is ready to deploy. In Round 4 (July 2026) no gate passed, and the 16-metal elastic result is a ceiling that needs the held-out answer. See →
  • That different models share one error direction on surface energies. In our 21-material benchmark, apparent alignment sat inside a coupling-aware null. See →
  • That a Lean check confirms the physics. See →
  • Anything about the newest multi-head or multi-fidelity models or their confidence heads. We have not tested them. See →
  • Peer review. No Lupine paper has been peer-reviewed or accepted yet. See →
