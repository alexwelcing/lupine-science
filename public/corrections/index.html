Corrections log · updated November 11, 2026

Corrections

When we retire a public claim, it is listed here with the date and the reason. The original wording is quoted so it can be checked against older copies; earlier versions of our pages also stay in our public git history.

For what we currently claim, with the scope attached, see What we can and cannot claim and Where it has been tested.

“Verified AI predictions”

Was
“Lupine Science — Verified AI predictions for materials discovery”; “makes AI predictions for new materials trustworthy”; “the trust layer” as a description of what we offer. Homepage title, share cards, company description.
Now
Lupine Science measures where machine-learned models of atoms go wrong, against published references and pass/fail gates set in advance, and publishes every verdict, failures included.
Why
Our evidence supports measurement, not verification of predictions. In four preregistered campaigns in July 2026 (Round 4), no gate passed: three failed and one abstained.
Retired
November 11, 2026

Runtime correction as a product

Was
“corrects that error at runtime as a physical field beside the model”. Company description, structured data and fact sheet.
Now
Correction is a research question here, not a product: it has held only in narrow, stated cases.
Why
The one correction that held blind is narrow: one model, two metals, one facet. Both Round-4 correction tests failed.
Retired
November 11, 2026

“~70% of dominant error directions are shared”

Was
“~70% of dominant error directions are shared across potentials”; “the models agree on direction about 69% of the time”; “errors span only 1.1–2.0 directions, not the thousands possible”. Homepage panels and their in-browser captions.
Now
Retired as evidence. The homepage background is labelled as an illustration drawn from four classical potentials. In our machine-learned potential benchmark, apparent cross-model alignment sat inside a coupling-aware null, so we do not count it as a result.
Why
The figure was a mean cosine over the six pairs of four classical (EAM) potentials, shown as a frequency and as if it described machine-learned potentials. It did neither.
Retired
November 11, 2026

Theorem counts as evidence

Was
“899 build-locked theorems in the open Lean library, zero sorry”; “every number reported is sealed as a machine-checked theorem”. Homepage counter, llms.txt, company description.
Now
Lean 4 checks the arithmetic and stated inequalities behind published numbers; it does not check the physics, the reference data or the pipeline. A generated count is not shown as a headline.
Why
A count of checked statements says nothing about whether the physics or the reference data are right.
Retired
November 11, 2026

“17.84 → 9.92 GPa” and a usable class-aware correction

Was
Elastic-constant error “17.84 → 9.92 GPa”, and a class-aware correction with “zero no-harm violations” in leave-one-element-out tests, presented as a usable correction.
Now
On 16 elemental cubic metals with four MatPES MLIPs (128 cases; r2SCAN targets approximate), one bias direction per functional transfers out of sample. When the held-out reference sets how far to correct, MAE falls from 17.84 to 10.36 GPa: a ceiling, not a deployable correction. A correction that works without seeing the answer is still open, and derived moduli are uncertified.
Why
The 9.92 GPa figure was an in-sample recalibration, and the 10.36 GPa figure needs the held-out answer to set the size of the correction. Neither is a correction you could apply to a new material.
Retired
November 11, 2026

“5–12 GtCO₂/yr” as a Lupine outcome

Was
“Five Materials for 5–12 GtCO₂/Year”, presented as a climate potential our work would unlock. Homepage, articles and investor material.
Now
Climate and environmental materials are one lane of where the work is aimed, marked Direction. Literature estimates appear only attributed to their sources, never as our outcome.
Why
The range was a sum of literature estimates for five material targets. Lupine Science has not measured or enabled any climate outcome.

Capability claims in the environmental series and prospectuses

Was

These pieces describe where the work is aimed (status: Direction). Their capability statements are hypotheses to test, each pointing to the result that bears on it.

The TMS2027 abstract’s universal “hyper-ribbon”

Was
The accepted TMS2027 poster abstract described a universal error “hyper-ribbon” that carries from classical potentials to machine-learned ones and makes transferability predictable.
Now
A poster abstract was accepted for TMS2027 (Orlando, 14–18 March 2027). It is a conference presentation, not a paper. Our record no longer supports the universal hyper-ribbon, and the proceedings manuscript replaces it with narrower measurement claims.
Why
Registered stress tests did not support it. Classical alignment did not predict foundation-model alignment, and a global elastic correction made results worse.
Retired
November 11, 2026

72.4% shown without its baseline

Was
“72.4% fewer DFT evaluations when four models share anchors”, on the homepage, in the company record and on cards, without its baseline.
Now
Shown only on the shared DFT anchors page, as projected accounting with no new DFT: 154 instead of 558 projected DFT evaluations on 29 analyzable paths (72.4% fewer). The baseline is four separate runs; paths have 5–7 images, so the union covers most images and deduplication alone caps the saving near 73%.
Why
Without the baseline, a projected count read as a measured saving.
Retired
November 11, 2026

“All four models under-predict every barrier”

Was
Round-4 and Z1 panel pages said every model under-predicted the migration barriers, systematically, including all 26 completed paths of one model.
Now
Under-prediction is model-dependent: at float64, CHGNet 25 of 28, MACE-MP-0 medium 21 of 29, MACE-MP-0 small 17 of 26 and MACE-MPA-0 medium 13 of 28 paths. The gate fails on error size for all four. See Round 4.
Why
The 26-of-26 count came from the float32 run; the locked float64 records do not support a universal direction.
Retired
November 11, 2026
