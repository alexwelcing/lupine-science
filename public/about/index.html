How the research works

Measure
Compare MLIP predictions with published reference values and record where, and in which direction, they miss.
Test against gates
Write the pass/fail gate down before a test or a correction runs, and label any amendment made after results are seen. Correction is a research question here, not a product: it has held only in narrow, stated cases. In four preregistered campaigns in July 2026 (Round 4), no gate passed: three failed and one abstained.
Publish verdicts
Release the data, the gate and the verdict together: pass, fail or abstain. Lean 4 checks the arithmetic and stated inequalities behind published numbers; it does not check the physics, the reference data or the pipeline.

What we can and cannot claim

We can claim, with the scope attached:

  • For nine fcc metals, an error field fitted to three other observables predicted the never-fitted (110) surface-energy error of four foundation MLIPs at r = 0.906 across 36 model–material cells (material-clustered 95% CI 0.82–0.96; permutation null centred near 0.44; significant for three of the four models). Evidence →
  • Used as an energy-only correction beside CHGNet, the field cut blind (110) surface-energy error from 9.7% to 1.5% on nickel and from 28.0% to 13.7% on copper: one model, two metals, one facet. Evidence →
  • Across 21 materials and 228 published reference values, four foundation MLIPs ranked surface energies almost correctly (Spearman 0.88–1.00) while missing magnitudes by tens of percent. Evidence →
  • On 16 elemental cubic metals, 1×1×1 TensorNet/PBE cells matched 3×3×3 cells for elastic-constant error (14.55 vs 14.61 GPa MAE). Evidence →

We cannot claim:

  • That any correction we have tested is ready to deploy. In four preregistered campaigns in July 2026 (Round 4), no gate passed: three failed and one abstained. On the 16-metal elastic panel, 17.84 → 10.36 GPa is a ceiling that needs the held-out answer, not a deployable correction. See →
  • That different models share one error direction on surface energies. In our 21-material benchmark, apparent cross-model alignment sat inside a coupling-aware null. See →
  • That a Lean check confirms the physics. Lean 4 checks the arithmetic and stated inequalities behind published numbers; it does not check the physics, the reference data or the pipeline. See →
  • Anything about the newest multi-head or multi-fidelity models or their confidence heads. We have not tested them. See →
  • Peer review. No Lupine paper has been peer-reviewed or accepted yet. See →

Public claims we have retired are listed, with dates and reasons, in the corrections log.

How Lupine works

Lupine Science is founder-led (Alex Welcing, founder). The work is done with AI coding and research agents under rules that live in code. AI assistance is disclosed, and a human author is responsible for every public summary. The rules, and how far each reaches:

  • In the research workbench, a pull request that changes a status in the assumption registry, the status-event snapshot or the locked ontology must bring a new evidence-bundle hash, or CI fails. The check does not read claim files, the Markdown ledgers or article status lines; those are reviewed by hand.
  • Public research-activity records pass a reviewed import that rejects local paths, emails, private URLs and credential-shaped strings; failed or stopped work cannot be relabelled completed.
  • The model review stage runs with an explicit tool list: packet critiques run without tools, and literature jobs can search and fetch public web pages. It cannot start experiments, publish or schedule runs.
  • A model's score is a recommendation, not evidence.
  • Lean must build with no sorry, and theorem counts are generated, never typed.
  • Partner-facing evaluation is designed to run in an offline HPC lane on the partner's own hardware: local manifests in, local JSON out, no outbound connections at runtime, telemetry off by default. That container has not yet been built on a facility cluster.

Where data goes. Agent model calls go to commercial model providers, through API keys and the providers' own signed-in command-line tools. Agent traces go to an Arize Phoenix tracing project. Benchmark runs use Lupine's cloud project or a local workstation.

Key-person risk is real: the program depends on one founder. Public data, code and the failure ledger mean the results can be checked without us. AI and data statement →

Common questions

What does Lupine Science do?

Lupine Science is an early-stage, founder-led research company. We independently measure the errors of machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs), test corrections against preregistered gates, record any amendment made after results are seen, and publish the verdict: pass, fail or abstain. Correction is a research question here, not a product: it has held only in narrow, stated cases. Nothing we have published is peer-reviewed yet.

What problem does it address?

Machine-learned potentials now screen far more candidate materials than any lab can test, and a model can be confident and wrong. Before a lab commits a year to a candidate, it needs to know where the underlying model is likely to miss. We measure that against published references, under gates set in advance, and publish the verdicts.

How is this different from building another interatomic potential?

Lupine Science does not build a potential and has no correction product. We measure existing models against published references under preregistered gates. Corrections have held only in narrow, stated cases, and both Round-4 correction tests (July 2026) failed.

Is the work peer-reviewed?

No Lupine paper has been peer-reviewed or accepted yet. The TMS2027 poster abstract was accepted for presentation; it is a conference presentation, not a paper. Each article carries a status label and links the data it uses. Where a Lean check exists, it checks the arithmetic and stated inequalities behind a number, not the physics, the reference data or the pipeline.

Will the newest universal potentials, with their own uncertainty estimates, make this unnecessary?

They may change what needs testing, and we have not tested them yet. Our tests so far cover earlier universal potentials, not the newest multi-head or multi-fidelity models or their confidence heads. What they do not replace is an outside, preregistered measurement against published references: that is what a model's own uncertainty can be checked against, and agreement among models trained on similar data cannot certify accuracy. Our own record is a reason for care: in a preregistered test, our 90% conformal intervals reached only 86.7% coverage (Projection Law Round 2). We have not run a head-to-head on these newer models; a fair one would need a training-overlap audit against our locked panels.

Who does the work?

Lupine Science is founder-led. Alex Welcing, the founder, does the work with AI coding and research agents under rules that live in code; a human author is responsible for every public summary. "How Lupine works" on this page lists those rules and where data goes.
